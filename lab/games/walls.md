### Run the Walls (`walls`)

*Asked for on 26 November 2026: "a game in the manner of the wall-running, acrobatic palace adventures, when
one climbs the walls at a run, etc." — the moves of that kind of game only: its look, its names and its levels
are this world's own.*

A ruin of pale sandstone stands on a rock of its own in the sea of the south bay, at (258, 100) — 91 m east of
the tower of the spiral, 143 m from the cleft, water under all of it: a stepped citadel 45 × 57 m, a round
turret at each of its eight corners, a keep in its middle and a ruined drum over that, its crown 42 m over the
sea. **The way is the ruin**: it winds round it and inwards like a snail's shell — nine straight sides
(42, 30, 42, 22.5, 34.5, 15, 27, 7.5 and 19.5 m) turned left into one another by eight flights of worn steps —,
**300 m from its foot, 6 m over the sea, to the top of the tower, 28.7 m higher**, each lap 7.5 m inside the
one before and 8 m and more over it. So the wall is always on the left and the sky on the right, and the camera
rides on the open side, behind the runner and a little above (6.2 m back, 3.5 m out, 3.3 m up): nothing is ever
between it and the runner. Where a wall stands on the right as well — the two halls — it comes in over the middle
of the way before the runner is between the two, and goes out again after. One asks to play on **a
landing of timber** at the south-east end of the south beach, past the quay of *Robots Can See You*: planks on
six piles, 11 × 4.5 m (x 77…88, z 93.7…98.2), one level 2.8 m over the sea, out over the water from the bank
toward the ruin — a ramp of planks onto it from the bank at (80, 100.4), a rail of the fair's paint (cream posts
with a bulb on each, a red hand rail, a blue one), the board of the best times and the board of its sponsors
along the north rail of its landward half, one's own best time on the south rail, and **the arch at its seaward
end, at (86.6, 95.95), facing the ruin**. It is declared to the collision as a platform: one walks onto it from
the shore, and is put back under its arch after a run. 3.7 m of water lie between it and the quay (z ≤ 90); the
mine mouth is 15.2 m from the arch, the cleft 30 m, the arch of the robots 31 m. From the arch the line to the
ruin passes 6 m clear of the quay, and the tower of the spiral stands 10° to its left: it hides the north wing
of the ruin, not its keep.

**The stick and one button.** Stick up runs (7 m/s), down steps back, aside goes across the way (3.4 m wide: the
wall on the left, a kerb on the right — nobody falls off the side of a floor). The button jumps (1.5 m up,
4.9 m long at a run; from a standstill with the stick up it is a leap of 4 m). The rest is what the stone is
painted for, and the paint never changes its meaning:

- **a band of blue along a wall is run along**: jumped at at a run, it carries one on along it in a shallow arc
  for 1.15 s — 8.4 m, over gaps of 6.5 to 7.5 m that no jump crosses. One is taken by it from three quarters of
  a metre off the wall, and from 0.7 m before the band begins — and **one need not aim at the stone**: beside a
  band a jump is drawn to the wall, the stick straight up; only the stick pushed away refuses it. **On it, the
  button kicks off** to the other side, and for 0.8 s the kick carries one across whatever the stick says: to
  the band of the wall that stands there, in the two halls (the kick may come at any moment of 0.95 s). Pushed
  away from the wall, the stick lets go of it.
- **a face of green across the way is run up**: run straight at — or pushed at from a standstill at its foot —
  one runs two or three steps up it (6.2 m/s), catches its edge and pulls up (0.3 s). No button. Nine faces, 1
  to 3 m (one runs up 3.5 m). **An edge one falls just short of is caught** the same way, when within 1.3 m of
  the hands: the first hole of the course, 2.2 m, is crossed by who simply runs into it; the second, 2.8 m, is
  not.
- **a gold bar across a gap is swung round**: jumped into (it is caught from 0.75 m), one hangs from it for as
  long as one likes, and the button lets go: thrown 5.6 m at 7.8 m/s.
- **red hurts**: four blades — pendulums hung from beams out of the wall, swinging 2.1 to 2.5 m each way in 2.6
  to 2.8 s, each a red crescent on a rod, low over the floor (0.25 to 1.05 m) and open above, so that what is
  behind it stays in sight: one goes round it, waits for it, or jumps it at the right stride — and two fields
  of spikes that come up through a plate 42 % of 2.4 s, their points showing and trembling a
  moment before. All of it keeps time on the clock everybody shares.
- **pale cracked slabs give way** 0.6 s after one sets foot on them (twelve of them: one keeps running); **a
  ledge** 0.85 m wide along the wall is shuffled along at 3.4 m/s, the back to the stone — nobody steps off its
  side.

A quarter of a second of grace everywhere: on an edge after the last stone, on a wall after it has let go.

**Time winds back.** A fall (3.6 m under the last footing), a blade or a spike does not send one to a save: one's
robot goes back along its own path — it is drawn in the air as a line of light — to **the last sure footing**:
the last place one stood on a floor that stays, with a stride of floor before and behind and nothing red within
a metre. It takes a second and a half at the most, and the clock runs on: that is what a mistake costs. **Three
rewinds in reserve** (the line of the run shows them: ◆◆◇), filled again at each save; with none left, a fall is
the last save, the reserve full again. **Twelve saves**, one at the head of each stretch (at 2, 25, 61, 89, 109,
139, 169, 185, 211, 234, 269 and 285 m: 25.7 m apart on average, 35.5 at the most), each a line of gold and
white across the way, a gold banner down the wall and a plaque with its number and the name of the stretch. The
clock starts with the first step; the time that counts is the time from it to the top, falls and all.

Twelve stretches, easy first, then mixed: **First Steps** (a hole, a step one runs up, a wider hole), **Along
the Wall** (two gaps, a band along each), **Up the Wall** (faces of 2.6 and 3 m, then a hole with a higher floor
beyond: jumped and caught), **Wall to Wall** (a gap of 16 m: a band on the left, then one on a wall on the right
that no jump reaches from the floor), **The Bars** (two gaps, a bar over each), **Blades and Spikes**, **Giving
Way** (slabs, a hole in them), **The Ledge** (9 m of it over nothing, a blade that reaches it), **Run and
Climb** (off a band onto a landing 3 m long and up a face), **The Roofless Hall** (22 m of nothing: left wall,
right wall, left wall), **Thin Ice** (slabs that give way under a blade: one waits before them, not on them),
**The Tower** (a face, a bar that throws one onto a higher floor, the last face) — and a brazier of gold at the
top.

**How it looks.** Stone laid in courses, lighter towards its top (the light is painted into the vertices); under
every floor a blind arcade — pilasters down to the rock or to the terrace of the lap outside, a dark arch between
each two, a pale roll round it; dark arched windows high in the walls; vines down from the tops of the walls and
over the kerbs; striped awnings of the fair's cloth over six of the saves (only where the wall is high enough and
no band follows: the camera never has one before it); bunting from mast to mast along the tops of the outer
walls; merlons, some fallen. One group in the scene, `walls`, of 41,000 triangles in all; what never moves is
one merged piece (five materials); the four blades, the two fields of spikes and the twelve slabs are small
pieces of their own, moved only while somebody is within 320 m; the plaques of the saves and the slabs are drawn
from within 110 m only. The rock is grey, boulders on its shore; under the gaps of the outer lap one sees it,
4 m down and more, and under those of the inner laps the terrace of the lap outside.

**With others.** Several run at once, each on their own clock, and see each other where the network says they
are; nobody is solid. The blades and the spikes are the same for everybody at every moment. The slabs that give
way do so for who treads on them only: somebody else's slabs are whole.

**What the checks say** (`node tools/walls-test.mjs`, 34 of 34; its last line is the line of
`server/scores.js`): the place (water under all of it; the landing's deck over all the ground under it, the head of its ramp on the
bank, the quay well clear; the gate on it 15 m and more from every other arch, the line from it to the ruin clear
of the quay and beside the tower of the spiral); the plan (each lap at
least 8.1 m over the one outside it, the tallest band 6.6 m; the corners carry nothing but steps); the line from
the camera to the runner, followed all along the way and across it, passes clear of every turret and of the
walls that stand on the right; the saves; each move does what it says and its gap is a fall without it; each jump
may come at any moment of 0.6 to 0.85 s, the kick from wall to wall of 0.95 s; every blade and field of spikes
lets one by at a run for at least a quarter of a second at a stretch (on a floor a blade always leaves one side
of the way free: it is read and gone round; the spikes are jumped at any moment);
a fall winds back to a footing a stride before the edge, three times, then it is the save; a save fills the
reserve. **The runner that plays by itself** tries, on a copy of itself and with the same rules, what it could do
two seconds ahead — a lane, the button now or later, twice, three times — and never presses sooner than a
quarter of a second after deciding to: it reaches the top without a fall in **48.8 s** (17 presses), whenever it
starts. **A clumsy one** — a third of its presses never come, most of the others come 0.1 to 0.4 s late — falls
one to five times, is wound back each time, and reaches the top in 53 to 65 s. **One who knows nothing of it** — the stick straight up all the
way, a press at each edge, on a wall as it ends, on a bar after a moment, steering only to get onto the ledge,
blind to what is red — reaches the top too, in 49 to 55 s with two to eight falls: what is red costs rewinds, not
the run. Who stands still stays at the start; who only runs falls at the second hole. `heatOf(state)` is how far the run has come (it never goes back down after a
fall). The board: `walls: { order: 'asc', min: 38000, max: 1800000 }` — 38.3 s is the whole way at the speed a
bar throws one at. In the running game (`node tools/play-test.mjs walls`, 13 of 13) it is offered at its gate,
played by its bot to the top (49 s there), its score told and kept.

**What it does not do, and what nobody has seen.**

- **Nobody has played it by hand.** A bot proves the top can be reached with presses a quarter of a second
  late; it does not prove that the stick and the button feel right, on a keyboard or on a telephone, nor that it
  is a pleasure. A person's first clean run is a guess: a minute and a half to two minutes.
- **It has been seen only in still pictures**, taken in a headless browser while the bot ran: the camera's glide
  into a hall and out of it, the lean along a wall, the swing under a bar, the rewind itself — none of it has
  been watched in motion. The robot uses the stock clips (run, jump, walk): it does not put its hands on the bar
  nor on an edge.
- **The blades never force a wait**, and the spikes are simply jumped: what keeps time is read, not waited for.
  Only the blade over the ledge can shut the way, and the bot does not stop for it: it jumps its crescent.
- **The ruin has no walls for who is not playing**: it stands in water one may swim in, and a swimmer goes
  through its rock. One cannot walk in it: one is put on it by the game, and taken off.
- **The slabs fall for who treads on them only**, and the others' robots are shown where the network places
  them: whether their lean along a wall or their swing under a bar shows for the others has not been looked at
  (two browsers were never run together).
- **The landing has been seen in pictures, not walked.** The gate was first on the sand at (64, 84) — until the
  quay of the robots was built 6 m from it and across the line to the ruin —, then on the dark rock of the bank at
  (79, 99), which was steep, shaded and squeezed the camera; the landing takes it out over the water. The checks
  put the player on its deck (2.80 m over the sea) and find the boards' feet on it; nobody has walked the ramp
  from the bank by hand: the bank beside it is steep (2 m of rise in 3), and whether the shore path brings one to
  the head of the ramp without a scramble has not been tried. The rail is painted wood only: who steps past it
  swims. The follow camera on the deck has the two boards close on its left (picture 7).
- The action "Leave the ruin" is shown by the game under the middle of the picture, as in every game of this
  kind: it sits just under the robot.
- The save at 25 m lies where one takes off for the first band: who jumps before its line is saved only on the
  far side of the gap (a fall in between winds back to before the line all the same).
- **No columns to hop**, which the first sketch of the game named: a jump here is as long as the run before it
  (4.2 to 4.9 m), and landing on a column a stride long would ask for a press timed to a tenth of a second.
- No sound of its own: the game's jump, land, hit, checkpoint and victory.

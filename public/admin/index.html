Your key

The owner's key (the secret ADMIN_KEY — the one of /admin <key> in the chat), or the key of an administrator the owner has named. It stays in this tab only, and is forgotten when the tab is closed.

Releases

What is switched on, for everybody, as the game runs — no delivery needed. A switch that goes on is applied at once in every browser; one that goes off asks every page to refresh at a safe moment (never in the middle of a game, a payment or a sentence).

A word to every room

Rooms

The rooms that hold somebody right now. Per room: the chat, who one sees, the big red button. Shared by all: the boards of scores, announcements, what is released.

Rooms made by players

A room of one's own is bought by the month, by an account. It is reached by its link only, holds fifty, and has its own boards of scores next to the world's. When it is no longer paid for it falls asleep — nobody gets in, nothing is lost — and is freed thirty days later.

To try one out yourself: sign in to your account in the game (Settings), then open a room for it here — for nothing, with no subscription. Its boards of scores are on the page High scores.

How newcomers are shared out

The common rooms (Room 1, Room 2…) are filled by the door: a new one opens once every room that takes newcomers holds “a new room at”; while it holds fewer than “starting under”, every newcomer goes there; a room that has fallen to “quiet at” or fewer takes no newcomer, and its visitors are offered a livelier room — nobody is ever moved. Whoever comes back finds their room again. npm run sim:rooms plays a whole day with these numbers.

How many connections one address may have

A school, a café or a phone network share one address: these are wide on purpose, and only stop somebody who opens connections by the hundred. Whoever is over one of them is told so, and let in as soon as there is room. You — with your key — are never refused. 0 switches a limit off.

High scores

Every board at once: the first five of each game for the period chosen (the rest behind "Show all"). Refusing a line takes that name off the four lists of its board. A visitor without an account is on the list of the day only.

Scores the server did not believe

A score at a booth, in the flying theatre or at the arcade that would enter the first five of a list is played again by the server, from what the page wrote down while it was played. One that does not come out the same is not put on the board — its author still sees it there, and is told nothing: it waits here for you, with the reason. Take it puts it on the board after all; Let it go forgets it. (Nothing of the game itself is kept.)

Chat

What was said in the rooms, the newest first: the lines for everybody, those said to who stood nearby, the "important" ones — and the lines of those kept out in silence, which nobody else read. What is said to Billy the AI is not chat and is not here. On a line: Kick puts its author out at once (they can come back); To the common rooms sends them out of a room of its own; Ban… keeps them out — by their browser and the account it is signed in to. Their address and their browser are never shown here.

Keep out

By their browser — and the account it is signed in to, if any. An address is shared by a school, a café, a phone network: everybody behind it would be kept out too.

Change

What it shows, for everybody, at once. The sponsor is not asked.

Sponsors

What a sponsor sends is shown at once. Take down what should not be there: with the money back (a bid in full; of a place paid by the month, the month that is running) or without. A place paid by the month stops being charged either way.

VAT and invoices

With the VAT on, Stripe works out the VAT due on each sale — where the buyer is — out of the price, which includes it (a bid stays what is paid); a business can give its VAT number; and each payment gets an invoice, which Stripe e-mails to the buyer. First, in Stripe: Settings → Tax (the address of the company and its VAT registrations), Settings → Business → Public details (what the invoices show), and Settings → Customer emails: "Successful payments" (so that invoices are sent). If Stripe Tax is not set up at all, Stripe refuses the VAT: the shop sells without it, and this says so here. The rest is not checked from here — look at a first invoice to see that it carries the VAT. A payment given back (outbid, taken down) gets its credit note.

Tax code — the kind of thing sold, in Stripe's words
  • What a tax code is. It tells Stripe what kind of thing is sold, so that Stripe applies the VAT — or the sales tax — that kind of thing bears where the buyer lives. It changes no price: the tax is taken out of what is paid.
  • Stripe Tax first. A code does nothing until Stripe Tax is switched on in Stripe itself: Settings → Tax — the address of the company, the countries where it is registered for VAT, and that “preset product tax code”.
  • One code for everything. Today the code chosen here goes with all the fair sells: hats, the pass, rooms and sponsors' places alike. In truth a sponsor's place is advertising, not a piece of a game: in the European Union that seldom changes the VAT, in the United States it can. Giving the sponsors a code of their own would take a second choice here and a few lines on the server — ask for it if your accountant says so. Which code is right, and where the company must register, is for an accountant to confirm: this page is no tax adviser.

Refunds

Sales

Everything that was bought, the newest first: one line a purchase — what is paid by the month is one line, with its latest payment. Refund… gives a payment back through Stripe, all of it or a part of it; before anything moves, it tells you how much can go back and what becomes of what was bought. The money given back some other way is written on its line too: a bid that was outbid, a place taken down, a refund made in Stripe itself.

This list is kept from the day this page arrived. For what was sold before, Fetch what Stripe remembers reads Stripe's own list and adds what is missing here. It only fills the list — it gives nothing and takes nothing back — and can be pressed again whenever you like: nothing is doubled.

Refund

How much goes back
What happens to what was bought

The money goes back to the card that paid, through Stripe: it shows on their statement within a few days. They are told in the game at their next visit, and by e-mail where an address is known. A refund cannot be undone.

Kept out

By address, by browser, by account. From the game, "Who is here" does the same with one click. In silence: the visitor is not told and notices nothing — they play on, everybody sees them, but what they write is read by nobody, their scores show on their own screen only, and what they sponsor (a panel, a plaque) is shown to them alone.
